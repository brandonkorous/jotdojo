# Jotacular Design & Brand Handoff

**Status:** Canonical design direction  
**Purpose:** Brand, product, and landing-page guidance for designers, developers, and coding agents  
**Primary domain:** `jotacular.com`

---

# 1. Product Definition

Jotacular is a lightweight capture surface for ideas.

It is not a traditional notes workspace, productivity dashboard, or knowledge-management app.

The product should feel like a **digital bar napkin**: immediate, unstructured, human, and available exactly when a thought appears.

Users can capture thoughts by:

- Handwriting or drawing directly on a canvas
- Typing
- Voice
- Photo

Those notes can later be accessed by the user or by AI agents through MCP.

The important product behavior is:

1. A thought happens.
2. The user captures it quickly.
3. The user moves on.
4. Later, the user asks an AI agent to use that thought.
5. The agent retrieves the note through MCP and continues the work.

Jotacular should reduce the mental burden of organizing ideas at capture time.

> **Capture now. Use later.**

---

# 2. Core Product Metaphor

The internal product metaphor is:

> **A bar napkin for the AI age.**

This is not necessarily the primary public tagline, but it should guide product and design decisions.

A bar napkin is:

- Immediate
- Unstructured
- Disposable-feeling
- Human
- Idea-first
- Used without planning
- Valuable because of what was written on it, not because of the medium

Jotacular should preserve that feeling while adding persistence and agent access.

The product is effectively a **capture layer between a user's brain and their AI agents**.

---

# 3. Core Positioning

Jotacular should not be described as:

- An AI notes app
- A second brain
- A knowledge workspace
- A productivity OS
- A Notion competitor
- An AI dashboard

Jotacular is closer to:

- A universal thought-capture surface
- A persistent digital napkin
- A note canvas your AI can read later
- A lightweight memory layer for people and agents

The AI capability should feel secondary to the act of capture.

The product should still be valuable even if the user never thinks about MCP directly.

It works because handwriting, typing, voice, and photos all “land” in Jotacular.

> **Don't organize it. Just jot it.**

This communicates the behavioral difference between Jotacular and traditional note-taking products.

## Supporting message

> Write it, type it, say it, or snap it. Jotacular keeps your thoughts ready for you—and whatever AI you use next.

## Alternate product phrases

- Jot it now. Figure it out later.

- Jot it once. Use it everywhere.

- Your thoughts. Ready for your AI.
- Capture now. Think later.

# 5. Example Product Story

The landing page and demos should explain the product through real life.

Example:

A user is sitting at a lake and gets an idea for KanNinja.

They open Jotacular and quickly write:

> kanNINJA idea  
> AI should break projects into boards  
> maybe based on goals / dependencies  
> think about planning agent

They put the phone away.

Later at home, they open Claude or ChatGPT and ask:

> Turn the KanNinja idea I jotted down earlier into a product plan.

The agent retrieves the jot through MCP and continues the work.

This story should be favored over feature-heavy explanations.

# 6. Brand Personality

Jotacular should feel:

- Friendly
- Immediate
- Human
- Slightly playful
- Calm
- Modern
- Dependable
- Unstructured in a good way
- Confident without feeling corporate
- AI-ready without looking like an AI startup

A useful balance:

- **70%** capable / modern / polished
- **20%** playful / energetic

The product should have small moments of personality, but never become childish or distracting.

# 7. Canonical App Icon

The app icon is based on the word:

> **jot**

The icon should not use an abstract letter J, node diagram, brain, sparkle, robot, or network symbol.

## Primary icon construction

- Flat dark charcoal rounded-square background
- Lowercase white `jot`
- Mint dot over the `j`
- Violet hand-drawn underline beneath `jot`
- No gradients
- No glossy effects
- No bevels
- No 3D shading
- No heavy shadows

The underline should feel like a real quick pen stroke:

- Slightly imperfect
- Slightly asymmetric
- Friendly
- Human
- Not mathematically straight
- Not overly decorative

The mint dot should remain simple and flat.

## Primary dark icon

Background:
- Charcoal / near-black

Foreground:
- White `jot`
- Mint `j` dot
- Violet underline

## Light icon variant

Background:
- Warm white / paper

Foreground:
- Charcoal `jot`
- Mint `j` dot
- Violet underline

## Small-size guidance

At favicon and tiny UI sizes:

- Remove all shadows and depth
- Keep the underline as a single visible stroke
- Maintain strong contrast
- Avoid excessively thin underline weight
- Preserve the mint dot if legible
- Test at 16px, 24px, 32px, 48px, and 64px

The icon should remain recognizable because it literally says `jot`.

---

# 8. Primary Wordmark

The wordmark is:

> **jotacular**

## Wordmark rules

- Lowercase
- Rounded, friendly sans-serif
- Charcoal on light backgrounds
- White on dark backgrounds
- Mint dot over the `j`
- No underline
- No gradients
- No decorative network elements

The wordmark should remain restrained.

The icon carries the handwritten underline.

The wordmark carries only the mint dot as the identifying visual cue.

This creates a clear system:

- **jot** = action / app icon
- **jotacular** = brand / product name

---

# 9. Horizontal Lockup

The canonical horizontal lockup is:

**[jot app icon] + jotacular wordmark**

Spacing should be generous enough that the icon and wordmark do not visually collide.

Because the app icon already contains a mint dot, test both of these lockups:

1. Icon + wordmark with mint dot
2. Icon + fully monochrome wordmark

The standalone wordmark should always retain the mint dot.

Use whichever combined lockup feels less visually repetitive at small sizes.

# 10. Typography

## Primary heading typeface

**Nunito Rounded**

This should be the main brand heading font.

Reasons:

- Friendly
- Rounded
- Modern
- Human
- Approachable without feeling childish
- Matches the soft geometry of the icon

Use for:

- Hero headings
- Section headings
- Marketing headlines
- Large callouts
- Brand-facing UI moments

## Body / UI typeface

Preferred:

**DM Sans**

Acceptable fallback:

**Inter**

Use for:

- Body copy
- Buttons
- Navigation
- Forms
- Product UI
- Metadata
- Tables
- Settings

## Handwritten accent typeface

Optional:

**Caveat**

Use sparingly.

Only for:

- Small annotations
- Human callouts
- Example scribbles
- Decorative “napkin” moments

Do not use Caveat for primary navigation or important UI text.

# 12. No Gradients

This is a strict brand rule.

> **Do not use gradients.**

Gradients are intentionally excluded because they are strongly associated with generic AI/SaaS visual language.

Do not use:

- Mint-to-violet gradients
- Purple-blue AI gradients
- Aurora effects
- Glow gradients
- Gradient text
- Gradient backgrounds
- Gradient borders
- Gradient buttons

Use flat colors and contrast instead.

---

# 13. Visual Language

The Jotacular visual language should come from the actual product.

## Primary cues

- Warm digital paper
- Subtle dot-grid canvas
- Ink
- Scribbles
- Underlines
- Loose circles
- Arrows
- Short annotations
- Imperfect marks
- Large areas of whitespace
- Floating lightweight tools

## Personality

Think:

> **Digital paper + a small spark of software**

Not:

> **AI dashboard + glowing network diagram**

The product itself is the visual inspiration.

---

# 14. Brand Pattern

A subtle dotted-grid pattern can be used as a recognizable background device.

It should resemble the actual Jotacular canvas.

Pattern guidance:

- Very low contrast
- Wide spacing
- Never visually noisy
- Warm/light on white surfaces
- Soft slate on dark surfaces

Occasionally combine with:

- Violet underline stroke
- Mint dot
- Hand-drawn loop
- Short handwritten note
- Small arrow

Avoid decorative node networks.

# 19. AI / MCP Messaging

MCP is the mechanism, not the brand promise.

Customer-facing language should prefer:

- Connect your AI
- Use your notes with any compatible agent
- Give your AI real context
- Jot once, use it anywhere

Technical pages can use:

- MCP server
- MCP-compatible agent
- Agent connection
- Context retrieval

Do not make the main brand identity dependent on MCP terminology.

---

# 20. Product UI Direction

The existing canvas aesthetic is a core part of the brand.

UI should preserve:

- Large open canvas
- Subtle dot grid
- Light warm background
- Floating toolbar
- Minimal navigation
- Pencil / marker controls
- Voice capture
- Camera capture
- Search
- Saved state

Avoid turning the product into:

- Inbox-heavy layouts
- Tag dashboards
- Traditional note lists
- Project-management sidebars
- Complex workspace navigation

Those features may exist, but they should not define the initial experience.

---

# 21. Motion & Microinteraction

Motion should feel light and physical.

Good motion:

- Ink stroke drawing in
- Underline appearing quickly
- Small mint dot pulse when saved
- Canvas note gently settling
- Voice waveform
- Photo dropping onto canvas
- Quick “saved” confirmation
- Agent retrieval shown as a note being surfaced

Avoid:

- Particle explosions
- Glowing AI effects
- Gradient sweeps
- Sparkles
- Floating network nodes
- Excessive spring physics

---

# 22. Brand Voice

Jotacular should speak like a capable friend.

Short.

Human.

Not corporate.

Examples:

Saved:

> Jot saved. Nice one.

Agent connected:

> Claude is now connected.

Nothing found:

> Hmm... not in your jots.

Empty state:

> Nothing here. Go have a thought.

Capture encouragement:

> Capture it now. Future you will thank you.

Use this personality sparingly.

# 23. Brand Do / Don't

## Do

- Use flat colors
- Use Nunito Rounded for brand headings
- Use DM Sans / Inter for body/UI
- Use the mint dot consistently
- Use the violet scribble underline in the icon
- Use warm paper and charcoal
- Use whitespace
- Use dot-grid canvas language
- Use human scribbles
- Show real capture moments
- Make the product feel immediate

## Don't

- Use gradients
- Use AI sparkles
- Use glowing nodes
- Use brains
- Use robots as a primary brand device
- Use futuristic holograms
- Make Jotacular look like Notion
- Make Jotacular look like a productivity dashboard
- Over-organize the UI
- Put the violet underline under the main Jotacular wordmark
- Add decorative network diagrams
- Use excessive 3D rendering

# 25. Design Principle

Every major design decision should pass this test:

> **Does this feel like something I would open for four seconds to save a thought and then forget about until I need it?**

If the interface requires the user to think about organization, workflows, structure, or AI before capturing the thought, it is moving away from the Jotacular product idea.

---

# 26. Final Brand Summary

Jotacular should feel like:

> A warm piece of digital paper that happens to be connected to the future.

The product is simple at the moment of capture and powerful later.

The brand should reflect that same balance:

- Human first
- Technology second
- Capture first
- Organization later
- Flat and timeless
- Friendly but capable
- Playful but controlled
- AI-connected without looking like “AI software”

**The thought is the hero. Jotacular is simply where it lands.**
