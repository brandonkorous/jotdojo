## The person

Hazel Trickett, 61, she/her. School secretary for twenty-six years, retired in
2024, and now the person who holds the diary for Stoke Prior village hall because
nobody else would. She does it on a Moto G with a cracked corner and reading
glasses she keeps losing.

**Technical level: low, and not apologetic about it.** She uses WhatsApp, the
Tesco app, and email. She has never used ChatGPT or Claude and has no plan to. She
knows what a password is and resents being asked for a new one.

**What she is nervous about.** Double-booking the hall. She did it once, in
February, and two families turned up for the same Saturday.

**What made her look today.** Her paper diary got rained on in the car park and the
April page is unreadable.

## The business

**Stoke Prior village hall** — one room, one kitchen, one car park, £18 an hour.

- About 90 bookings a year, plus five regular weekly groups
- Everything currently lives in a paper A5 diary and her own memory
- **Inconvenient for the software:** she does not read screens, she scans them, and
  she will not scroll to find something she expected to see. If it is not in the

## Run log

### 2026-09-16 — act 1, she reads the marketing site as a 61-year-old

**The act's `major` condition is NOT met, and that is the headline.** The site does
make sense to somebody who has never used an agent. Recorded as a pass, because it
was the thing most likely to be wrong.

**What lands for her**, sentence by sentence:

- **"Don't organize it. Just jot it."** The headline, and the best possible one for
  a woman whose paper diary got rained on.
- *"The cursor is already blinking. Nothing to name, nowhere to file it, no
  new-note button to hunt for first."*
- **The whole "You have tried the other ones" band.** *"There is a note-taking app
  on your phone right now with four things in it, two of them from 2023. It is not
  a bad app. It asked you to keep a system going, and systems are a thing you
  maintain on a good week."* — and then *"Filing was never what made a note worth
  keeping. Finding it again was."* This is the band that tells Hazel the product is
  for her, and nothing in it mentions an agent.
- *"Free is a plan here, not a trial with a fuse in it."*
- The examples: **"Under the sink — the size printed on the filter, so you buy the
  right one this time."** That is her life, not a founder's.

**What means nothing to her**, also sentence by sentence:

| Where | What she reads |
| --- | --- |
| The browser tab | **"Jotacular — the notes app Claude can read"** |
| Hero subhead, last clause | "and whatever AI you use next" |
| A whole band | "Connect your AI" · "Paste one link into Claude's settings. Or ChatGPT's." |
| End of that band | "Jotacular works with **MCP-compatible agents**" |
| The lake story | "the plan is Claude reading it through a connection set up once" |
| **Pricing** | "Five dollars a month when you want **Claude writing back as well as reading**" |
| The footer line | "Your notes, readable by your AI, from your phone, with no computer running" |

**The two that matter are the tab title and the price.** The title is the only
sentence that travels — it is what a search result and a shared link show — and it
defines the product by the one thing she does not have. And **the sole stated reason
to ever pay is an agent writing back**, so there is no path from free to paid that
means anything to her, ever.

**Neither is filed as a defect.** The title is honest positioning and it is exactly
how P01 found the product; the price difference is a real product decision in
docs/01. They are recorded here because act 1 asked which sentences mean nothing to
her, and because a village hall secretary being a permanently free customer is a
fact worth Brandon seeing written down rather than inferring.

**MCP is not filed either.** It appears once, at the end of the band she is already
skipping, with a link that explains it. The marketing site is allowed a technical
audience in a technical band.

#### Filed and fixed: issue 026 — the button that said "Start jotting" asked her to sign in

She did not read the page first. She scanned it and tapped the biggest green thing,
which is what she does, and it took her to a **sign-in screen** — two inches below
the sentence *"Nothing to sign up for. Start typing or writing."*

All four **Start jotting** buttons linked to the app, and `/` on the app host with no
draft cookie redirects to `/signin`. It works for anybody who typed in the hero box
first, which is why P01 never hit it.

Fixed by giving the two words two jobs: **Start jotting** now puts the caret in the
canvas under it, the closing band points at that same box, and the header says
**Open the app**, which is what it does and what the footer has always called it.

### 2026-09-16 — act 2, she signs up and gets nothing explained

Straight through, and the spine held for a second persona.

**Issue 005's fix works for her too** — the sign-in screen said *"Your jot is
waiting. Signing in is what keeps it."* That is a regression check on a P01 fix
(RULE #7), and it passed.

Her jot came across word for word, `£50` included, into one space called `Personal`.

**What she thinks she is looking at:** a piece of paper with her own sentence on it

**A harness lesson, not a product defect.** Signing in inside a cross-site iframe —

`SameSite=Lax`. The server did everything right; the browser dropped the cookie. The
harness must sit on the same host as the app once a persona leaves the apex.

### 2026-09-16 — the export, which is her whole future

Her standing check is the one that decides whether this product is worth starting:
**six months from now, could she hand the hall diary to whoever takes over?**

**Filed and fixed: issue 027.** The zip is well made — a README that explains itself,
one markdown file per note named after its own first line, nothing lost. Then every
single note said:

> `_[handwritten, nothing legible on it]_`

Hazel has never handwritten anything. A canvas creates its ink layer the moment it
opens, and the renderer could not tell an untouched layer from a bad drawing — so
**every typed canvas note, for every customer, exported as illegible handwriting**,
and MCP's `get_note` said the same thing to any agent.

Fixed at the renderer, fed by both block loaders, plus the attachment label, which
called a picture of her typed page "handwriting". Her export now reads exactly as
she would want to hand it over.

### 2026-09-16 — act 7, the verdict

**She keeps using it, and she would still be using it in June.**

The reason is narrow and it is not the one on the tin. The hall diary is fourteen
sentences she needs to find again on a phone in a car park, and this product does
exactly that in two taps with no filing, no folders, no titles and no system to
maintain. `4 apr` finds the christening. `extinguisher` finds the certificate. Her
own shorthand works because nothing asked her to change it.

What she would say if asked: *"It is a notebook that does not lose things and does
not tell me off. I do not know what the Claude part is and I have not needed it."*

**And the part Brandon should read twice.** Everything that makes this product
special to its own marketing site — the agent, the connection, the five-dollar plan
— she will never touch. **She is a free customer forever, by design**, and the
product is genuinely good for her anyway. Whether that is a healthy funnel or a
costly one is a business question, not a defect, and it is the thing only this
persona could show.

The things that nearly stopped her were both fixed today: a button that asked her to
sign up after promising she would not have to (026), and an export that called her
typing illegible handwriting (027). Without those two she would have gone back to
paper on day one and blamed herself for it.
